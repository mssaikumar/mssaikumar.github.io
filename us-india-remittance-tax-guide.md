# US ⇄ India Remittances — Tax & Compliance Guide

> Last updated: 2026-09-18 · Verified against current law

**Scope of this doc:** moving money in either direction — the new 1% US remittance excise tax, gift tax both sides, and repatriation back out of India. Reporting the Indian accounts the money lands in: `US India Tax Compliance.md`. How India taxes the income those funds earn: `NRI Taxation Overview.md`.

---

## What changed recently (2025–2026)

- **New 1% US remittance excise tax (IRC §4475, OBBBA) — effective for transfers after 31 Dec 2025.** It only hits transfers funded with **cash, money orders, cashier's checks, or similar physical instruments**. Transfers funded from a **US bank account or a US-issued debit/credit card are exempt** — which is how you already remit, so your Wise/Remitly/bank-wire flow costs 0%. Collected by the provider at the time of transfer. (https://kahnlitwin.com/blogs/tax-blog/new-1-remittance-tax-what-the-obbba-means-for-cross-border-transfers)
- **2026 gift tax:** annual exclusion **$19,000/recipient**; non-citizen-spouse annual limit **$194,000**; lifetime exemption **$15,000,000** — made permanent by OBBBA, inflation-indexed from 2027.
- **India TCS on LRS (India→US direction):** threshold **₹10 lakh/FY** (Budget 2025, up from ₹7L); Budget 2026 cut education/medical/tour rates to **2% from 1 Apr 2026**; other purposes stay **20% above ₹10L**; nil for education funded by an 80E loan.
- **Form 15CA/15CB become Forms 145/146** under the Income-tax Act 2025 for remittances on or after 1 Apr 2026.

## 1. US → India: sending money (your regular direction)

**There is no US income tax on sending your own after-tax money to India.** A transfer is not a taxable event. The three things that *can* attach:

| Concern | When it applies | Your exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **1% §4475 excise** | Only cash/money-order-funded transfers from 1 Jan 2026 | **Zero** if funded from your bank account/debit/credit card. Never hand cash to a transfer agent. |
| **Gift tax (Form 709)** | Only if the money becomes a **gift to someone else** > $19,000/person/year | See §2 |
| **Provider reporting** | Transfers are reported under BSA rules by the provider; nothing for you to file | Keep records; structuring (splitting to dodge reporting) is itself a crime |

**India side of an inbound remittance:** money you send **to your own NRE/NRO account is not income** — no Indian tax on the transfer itself. FEMA places no ceiling on inward personal remittances. Park long-term savings in **NRE** (repatriable, interest tax-free while NRI) rather than NRO (interest taxed at 30%+ TDS).

## 2. Gifts to family in India

**US side (you're the donor — donor pays, recipient never does):**
- ≤ $19,000 per recipient per year (2026): nothing to file. You and a spouse can jointly give $38,000 per recipient.
- Above that: file **Form 709** — almost never actual tax, it just debits your **$15M lifetime exemption**.
- **Gift to your (non-citizen) spouse:** unlimited marital deduction does *not* apply; annual limit **$194,000** (2026) before 709 is needed.
- Paying a parent's hospital/tuition bill **directly to the institution** is unlimited and exclusion-free.

**India side (recipient):** gifts from "**relatives**" under §56(2)(x) — parents, siblings, spouse, and their spouses, lineal ascendants/descendants — are **fully exempt, any amount**. Gifts from non-relatives are tax-free only up to ₹50,000/year aggregate; beyond that the *whole* amount is the recipient's income at slab. A gift deed / bank-trail documentation is cheap insurance for large transfers.

**Example:** you send parents $60,000 in 2026 for a home repair. US: $19k+$19k (father+mother) excluded; file Form 709 for the ~$22k excess — no tax, lifetime exemption dips to ~$14.978M. India: parents are relatives — ₹0 tax, any amount. Net tax: zero; paperwork: one form.

**Money you *lend* (not gift) in India:** no gift tax, but interest (actual or AFR-imputed) is taxable income to you in both countries and the note itself is Form 8938-relevant — see `US India Tax Compliance.md` §1 and §3.

## 3. Gifts/inheritance coming TO you in the US

- **Form 3520** if you receive **> $100,000** in a year from a foreign person (aggregate gifts/bequests from parents count together). No US tax — pure disclosure — but the penalty for skipping it is up to 25% of the gift.
- India side: your parents gifting *you* is also §56(2)(x)-exempt (lineal descendant). If they remit it from India, **their** transfer runs under LRS and faces **20% TCS above ₹10 lakh** (creditable/refundable in their ITR) — factor that cash-flow hit before asking them to wire large sums.

## 4. India → US under LRS (the other direction — e.g., parents, or you pre-departure)

- LRS cap: **$250,000 per person per FY** for Indian residents (not applicable to you while NRI).
- TCS (from 1 Apr 2026): nil below ₹10 lakh; **2%** education/medical/tour; **20%** investment/other above ₹10L; nil for 80E-loan-funded education. TCS is a prepaid credit, not a cost — recovered in the sender's Indian ITR.
- **Your NRO/NRE repatriation is NOT LRS** and attracts **no TCS** — see §5.

## 5. Repatriating from India to the US (and again after you eventually return-return)

- **NRE/FCNR balances:** freely repatriable, no limit, no forms beyond the bank's.
- **NRO balances** (rent, Indian dividends, sale proceeds, loan repayments to you): repatriable up to **USD 1 million per financial year**, with a CA certificate — **Form 15CB + your 15CA** (Forms **146/145** from 1 Apr 2026). Above $1M/yr needs RBI approval. Tax on the underlying income must be settled first.
- Loan repayments from friends/family land in NRO (principal is not income; the interest is — both countries).
- Property-sale proceeds follow the same NRO $1M route; up to two residential-property sale proceeds are repatriable.

## 6. Action items / decision points

1. **Keep every remittance bank-account-funded** — the 1% excise tax is entirely avoidable and only ever hits cash-type funding. Never use cash at an agent counter.
2. **Route new savings-type remittances to NRE, not NRO** — repatriability + tax-free interest while NRI; NRO is only for India-source receipts.
3. **Track gifts per recipient per calendar year**; file Form 709 when any one person crosses $19,000 (or spouse $194,000) — it costs nothing but protects the record against the $15M exemption.
4. **If parents ever send you > $100,000 in a year: calendar Form 3520** with your 1040 — the missed-form penalty is vicious for a no-tax disclosure.
5. **Before moving back:** repatriate or redesignate deliberately — NRO's $1M/yr cap means a large India corpus takes multiple years to move; conversely, money you'll keep in India post-return should go through the RNOR/RFC planning in `NRI Taxation Overview.md` §5.
6. **Watch:** IRS implementation guidance on §4475 (provider practices are still settling in 2026); whether Budget 2027 tweaks TCS again; the 145/146 e-filing workflow after Apr 2026.
