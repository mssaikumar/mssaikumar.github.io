# Tax Implications of Rewards, Cashback & Bonuses

> Last updated: 2026-09-18

US tax treatment of cashback (Rakuten, TopCashback, credit-card rewards), sign-up/referral bonuses, and bank bonuses. Written for a **US resident alien (H-1B)**; a short nonresident-alien section covers the post-India-move scenario. Educational reference, not tax advice.

## What changed recently (2025–2026)

- **OBBBA (enacted 4 Jul 2025) killed the $600 1099-K saga.** The threshold reverted to **$20,000 AND 200 transactions**, retroactively — so the planned $5,000 (2024) / $2,500 (2025) / $600 (2026) phase-down is dead. Applies for tax years 2025 and 2026 onward. (Some states still have their own lower 1099-K thresholds.) https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000
- **1099-NEC / 1099-MISC threshold rises $600 → $2,000** for payments made on or after 1 Jan 2026, indexed for inflation from 2027 (first change since 1954).
- **The substantive doctrine is unchanged.** No new IRS guidance in 2024–2026 on credit-card rewards or cashback: purchase-linked rewards are still nontaxable rebates; no-purchase bonuses are still income. Higher form thresholds change *paperwork*, never *taxability*.
- Crypto: **Rev. Rul. 2023-14** (staking rewards = income at dominion & control) still stands as of Sept 2026, though Treasury's 2025 digital-asset report recommended revisiting it — watch this space.
- Context: OBBBA also raised the 2025 standard deduction (single ≈ **$15,750**), which is what actually shelters small side income for residents.

## The core rule: rebate vs. income

Did you have to **spend your own money** to get it? Then it's a price reduction (rebate), not income. No spend → income.

| Reward | Taxable? | Form you might get | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit-card cashback / points on purchases | **No** (rebate) | None | Includes Amex MR earned on spend |
| Credit-card sign-up bonus **requiring spend** (e.g., 60k after $6k) | **No** (rebate) | None | Purchase-linked |
| Rakuten / TopCashback **shopping** cashback | **No** (rebate) | None | Reduces your cost basis; if you *deduct* the purchase (business), reduce the deduction |
| Rakuten / cashback-site **referral** bonus | **Yes** | 1099-MISC if ≥ $600 (2025) / ≥ $2,000 (2026+) | Taxable even with no form |
| **Bank account opening bonus** | **Yes** | 1099-INT (banks issue at ≥ $10) | Treated like interest; no-spend = income |
| Brokerage transfer/opening bonus | **Yes** | 1099-MISC | Same logic |
| Survey / task / GPT-app earnings (Swagbucks etc.) | **Yes** | Usually none under thresholds | "Other income"; gift-card payout counts at face value |
| Airline miles from **flying** | No (IRS Announcement 2002-18 forbearance) | None | Long-standing non-enforcement |
| Prize / sweepstakes / promo with no purchase | **Yes** | 1099-MISC ≥ threshold | FMV at receipt |
| Crypto **rewards card** cashback (paid in crypto) | No at receipt (rebate) | Broker 1099-DA on later sale | Rebate sets cost basis; selling later = capital gain/loss |
| Crypto **staking** rewards | **Yes** at dominion & control | Varies | Rev. Rul. 2023-14; FMV when you can sell/transfer |

Two evergreen principles:
1. **All income is reportable regardless of forms.** A $300 referral bonus with no 1099 is still taxable. Thresholds only control what the payer tells the IRS.
2. **Payment method is irrelevant.** PayPal, check, statement credit, or Amazon gift card — same character, FMV at receipt.

Edge case: manufactured-spend-style abuse (buying cash equivalents with rewards cards) can flip "rebate" into income — *Anikeev v. Commissioner* (T.C. Memo 2021-23). Normal spending is nowhere near this line.

## Reporting thresholds cheat sheet (payer → IRS)

| Form | Threshold | As of |
|---|---|---|
| 1099-K (PayPal, Venmo goods & services, marketplaces) | **$20,000 AND 200 transactions** | 2025+ (OBBBA, retroactive; check state rules — a few states use $600–$2,500) |
| 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC | **$600** through 2025; **$2,000** from 2026, indexed after | Federal |
| 1099-INT (bank bonuses, interest) | **$10** — unchanged; bank bonuses effectively always reported | Federal |
| W-2G, 1099-DA, etc. | Own regimes | — |

## How to report (resident alien = same as citizen)

- Worldwide income, Form 1040, standard deduction available.
- Referral/survey/bonus income that isn't a business → **Schedule 1, line 8 "other income."** No self-employment tax on hobby-level, sporadic amounts.
- If it becomes systematic and profit-seeking → Schedule C, and SE tax (15.3%) applies above $400 net. For a few hundred dollars of referrals a year, "other income" is the defensible spot.
- Bank bonuses land on Schedule B with interest.
- Keep a simple yearly log split into **rebates (not reported)** vs **income (reported)** — critical if a platform ever sends a 1099-K lumping both.
- Don't chase "stay under $600/$2,000 per payer" as a *tax* strategy — it changes paperwork, not liability. It does reduce mismatch-notice risk, nothing more.

## After a permanent move to India (nonresident alien years)

Short version — the switch from resident to NRA changes *scope*, not the rebate doctrine:

| Item | NRA treatment |
|---|---|
| Scope | US-source income only; Indian income invisible to the US (but India will tax worldwide income once you're ROR there — see `NRI Taxation Overview.md`) |
| US bank **interest** | **Exempt** for NRAs (deposit-interest exclusion, IRC §871(i)); bank reports on 1042-S, no tax |
| US bank **bonuses** | US-source FDAP-type income — default **30% withholding**, reported on **1042-S** (India–US treaty gives no blanket reduction for this) |
| Referral/reward income earned while physically in India | Services performed outside the US are generally **foreign-source** → not US-taxable (India taxes it instead) |
| Filing | 1040-NR if you have US ECI or under-withheld US income; **no standard deduction** (the India-treaty standard deduction is students/trainees only, not H-1B alumni); no SE tax for NRAs |
| Departure year | **Dual-status return** — resident through departure, NRA after; plan the move date |
| Housekeeping | File **W-8BEN** with every US bank/broker after the move; expect some banks to close accounts over foreign addresses |

Practical upshot: before the move, harvest any planned bank bonuses **while still a resident** (graduated rates + standard deduction beat 30% flat withholding), and stop US-side referral churning after it.

## Action items

1. **Keep one running log per calendar year**: column A rebates (Rakuten shopping, card cashback — not reported), column B income (referrals, bank bonuses, surveys — reported even with no 1099).
2. **Expect fewer forms from 2026** (1099-K back to $20k/200; 1099-MISC/NEC at $2,000) — report column B anyway; the IRS FAQ is explicit that taxability is unchanged.
3. **Bank-bonus churning is fine and simple while resident**: it's interest income at your marginal rate; no SE tax, no special forms beyond 1099-INT.
4. **Before any India move**: front-load bank/brokerage bonuses into your final resident year, file W-8BENs after departure, and get the dual-status year planned with a preparer.
