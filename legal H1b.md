# Side Income on an H-1B: What's Safe, Gray, and Prohibited

> Last updated: 2026-09-18

The core rule has not changed: **an H-1B holder may only work for the petitioning employer(s)**. Income that arrives without you performing labor (passive income) is fine; income earned by doing work — coding, tutoring, consulting, content creation — for anyone other than an H-1B sponsor is unauthorized employment, no matter how the payment is labeled. What *has* changed since 2025 is the enforcement climate and the machinery around the visa itself.

---

## What changed recently (2025–2026)

- **$100K fee proclamation — currently BLOCKED.** Proclamation 10973 (signed Sept 19, 2025) imposed a $100,000 fee on *new* H-1B petitions for beneficiaries **outside the US without a valid H-1B visa**. USCIS guidance (Oct 2025) exempted extensions, amendments, employer transfers, and change-of-status petitions for people already in the US — existing H-1B holders never owed it, including for travel on a valid visa/approval. On **June 8, 2026** the District of Massachusetts vacated the fee as an unlawful tax; on **July 24, 2026** the First Circuit refused to stay that ruling, so **the fee is not being collected as of Sept 2026**. The merits appeal is **still pending**, and the proclamation itself expires by its own terms on **Sept 20, 2026** unless extended. Watch: DHS is pursuing the same goal by regulation instead (below).
- **Regulatory replacements in motion.** (1) Final rule effective **Sept 9, 2026**: the 9-11 Biometric Fee ($4,000/H-1B petition) now applies to extensions filed by "covered employers" (50+ US employees, >50% on H-1B/L-1) — this hits large outsourcing firms, not typical employers. (2) **Proposed** rule (Federal Register, Aug 25, 2026): a **$103,265 fee on cap-subject H-1B petitions** — a proposal only, in its comment period, **not in effect**; expect litigation if finalized.
- **Weighted (wage-based) lottery — FINAL and in effect.** Final rule published Dec 29, 2025, effective Feb 27, 2026, first used for the **FY2027 cap season (March 2026 registration)**. Entries are weighted by wage level: Level IV = 4 lottery entries, III = 3, II = 2, I = 1. Doesn't affect you directly (you're already counted against the cap; transfers, extensions, and concurrent H-1Bs for prior cap-subject holders skip the lottery), but it shapes who else gets in.
- **Dropbox is gone for H-1B.** Effective **Sept 2 / Oct 1, 2025**, the State Department eliminated interview waivers for H-1B worldwide. **Every H-1B visa stamping — including renewals — now requires an in-person consular interview**, with long appointment backlogs.
- **Social media vetting.** Since **Dec 2025**, H-1B and H-4 visa applicants are expected to set social media accounts to public for consular review (expanded to more categories Mar 30, 2026). The DS-160 requires disclosing all handles used in the past 5 years. Officers specifically look for posts inconsistent with your visa terms — **including evidence of unauthorized side work**.
- **H-1B modernization rule (effective Jan 17, 2025) still stands in 2026**: deference to prior approvals codified, cap-gap extended to April 1, revised specialty-occupation definition, and **stronger site-visit authority** — refusal or failure to verify facts at a worksite can lead to denial or revocation.

## Income types at a glance

| Income type | Rating | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends, interest, capital gains (stocks, ETFs, bonds, CDs) | GREEN | Personal investing is passive. Buy/hold/sell freely. |
| Crypto buying, holding, occasional selling | GREEN | Same as stocks. Report gains on taxes. |
| Credit-card rewards, cashback, points, bank sign-up bonuses | GREEN | Rebates/promotions, not compensation for work. Not even taxable in most cases (bank bonuses are). |
| Rental income (property you own, esp. with a property manager) | GREEN | Landlording as an investor is passive. Keep hands-on involvement minimal. |
| One-off personal sales (car, furniture, garage sale, used electronics) | GREEN | Disposing of personal property is not employment. |
| Prize winnings, lottery, casino, genuine hackathon/contest prizes | GREEN | Windfalls, not wages. One-offs only — not a strategy. |
| Gifts and inheritances | GREEN | Not income from work. File Form 3520 for large foreign gifts. |
| Bank/brokerage refer-a-friend bonuses (occasional) | GREEN | Treated like promotional bonuses when incidental. |
| Concurrent H-1B second job | GREEN | The one legal path to *active* side income — second employer files its own petition. |
| Frequent/day trading (stocks or crypto) | YELLOW | No bright-line rule; a pattern that looks like a trading *business* (high frequency, primary income, trading during work hours) risks being called unauthorized work. 2026 scrutiny is real. |
| Royalties from work created before US entry / outside the US | YELLOW | Generally defensible as passive; keep proof of when/where created. |
| LLC ownership as a purely passive investor | YELLOW | Owning is legal; *any* operational work for it is not. Document that others run it. |
| Multiple short-term rentals / self-managed Airbnb operation | YELLOW | Scale and personal labor turn an investment into a business. |
| Systematic referral/affiliate promotion (blogs, links, influencer-style) | YELLOW→RED | Actively promoting for commissions is work. |
| GitHub Sponsors / recurring donations for your open-source work | YELLOW→RED | Recurring payments tied to ongoing coding look like compensation for services. Unpaid open-source contribution as a hobby is fine — decline the money. |
| App store / ad revenue from an app you build or maintain in the US | RED | Monetizing a project you actively develop here is self-employment. (An unmonetized personal app for your own use is a hobby — fine.) |
| Paid tutoring, freelancing, Upwork/Fiverr, 1099 consulting, bug bounties | RED | Employment for a non-sponsor. Payment label doesn't matter. |
| Running/managing your own business (even "after hours") | RED | Any hands-on role in your own company is unauthorized work without a petition from that company. |
| "Volunteering" in a normally-paid role to skip the paycheck | RED | Unpaid work in a role that's ordinarily compensated is still unauthorized employment. |

## Passive investing: stocks and crypto

Personal investing with your own money is squarely allowed — dividends, interest, and capital gains require no work authorization. The line USCIS could draw is **passive investor vs. de facto trader**: dozens of trades a day, trading as your main income, or trading that resembles a business invites the unauthorized-employment argument. There is still no statute or regulation defining the threshold (as of 2026 this remains interpretive), so the practical guidance is unchanged: **invest, rebalance, even trade occasionally — don't run a trading operation**. Report everything on your 1040 (Schedule B/D, crypto question answered honestly). Clean tax records *help* you: they show passive income categorized as such.

## Rental income

Owning and leasing property is investment income. Safe pattern: long-term tenant, property manager or contractors handling maintenance, income reported on Schedule E. Risky pattern: a portfolio of self-managed short-term rentals where you clean, host, and message guests daily — that's operating a hospitality business.

## Cashback, rewards, and referral bonuses

Credit-card cashback and points are purchase rebates — no immigration dimension at all. Bank account sign-up bonuses are interest-like promotional income: legal, report on taxes (1099-INT). Occasional refer-a-friend bonuses from your bank or brokerage are in the same family. The gray zone starts when referral income becomes something you *work at* — content, audiences, affiliate funnels.

## Coding projects, open source, and your personal app

- **Building software for yourself, unmonetized** (like a personal finance app you alone use) is a hobby. No income, no problem — and it's résumé-legal.
- **Monetizing it while on H-1B** (App Store sales, ads, subscriptions, "buy me a coffee") crosses into self-employment, because you're actively developing in the US. The old "royalty" framing only helps for work **completed before you entered the US** — not for a project you keep shipping updates to.
- **Open source without money is fine.** GitHub Sponsors, Patreon, or Open Collective payouts tied to your ongoing maintenance are best avoided — recurring money for continuing code looks like compensation for services. A single unsolicited gift is lower-risk but still gray.
- **Options if you want the income:** (a) wait for an EAD/green card; (b) have the project's company sponsor a concurrent H-1B (heavyweight, only for a real venture); (c) if you move back to India, monetize freely from there — work performed outside the US is outside US immigration law entirely.

## Tutoring and teaching

Paid tutoring — online platforms, private students, per-session pay — is employment and prohibited without authorization. The two legal shapes: **volunteer tutoring** through a nonprofit/community program where *nobody* is paid for that role (no stipends, no gift cards), or a **concurrent H-1B** filed by a school/college for a part-time teaching role.

## Business ownership and LLCs

You may **own** equity — LLC membership, startup shares, limited-partner stakes — and receive **profit distributions as an investor**. You may not manage, code for, sell for, or do daily tasks for the business (even trivial ones, like paying vendors, have been flagged by attorneys). If you want to *work* for your own startup: structure it with a board/co-founders who can genuinely supervise and fire you, and have the company file its own H-1B petition. The 2025 modernization rule actually clarified that beneficiary-owners can qualify (with conditions, including initial validity limited to 18 months) — but this is attorney territory.

## Concurrent H-1B: the legal path to active side income

Unchanged in 2026 and still the only clean way to be *paid for work* outside your main job. A second employer files its own (usually part-time) H-1B petition; since you've already been counted against the cap, it skips the lottery. Each job must independently qualify (specialty occupation, LCA wage). Your primary employer's permission isn't legally required — but check your employment agreement; **financial institutions almost always require written approval for outside business activities (OBA)**, and violating that is a fireable compliance issue even when immigration law is satisfied.

## Consequences and the 2026 enforcement climate

Unauthorized work is a **status violation**: it can sink an extension, transfer, or change of status; surface at visa stamping (where every H-1B applicant now sits an in-person interview, with social media reviewed); and complicate a green card (for employment-based adjustment, INA 245(k) forgives up to 180 days of violations — a limited cushion, not a license). Enforcement pressure is measurably higher in 2026: expanded USCIS site visits under the modernization rule, consular social media vetting explicitly looking for unauthorized-employment signals since Dec 2025, and cross-checking of tax records (a 1099-NEC for freelance work is a bright red flag in your own file). Never hide income from the IRS — the violation is the unauthorized work, not the reporting; unreported income makes both problems worse.

## Travel in late 2026

- With a **valid, unexpired H-1B visa stamp**, travel is normal — the $100K fee never applied to existing holders re-entering, and it's currently vacated anyway.
- If your **stamp is expired**, budget serious lead time: no dropbox anywhere in the world since Oct 1, 2025, in-person interview required, appointment backlogs of months at Indian consulates, and social media vetting (public profiles, 5 years of handles on the DS-160). Avoid non-essential trips that require a new stamp; carry recent pay stubs and your approval notice.
- Re-check the fee landscape before any consular appointment: the proclamation expires Sept 20, 2026, the appeal is live, and the proposed $103,265 regulation could change filings again if finalized.

## Action items

1. **Keep doing freely:** index/stock/crypto investing, credit-card rewards optimization, bank bonuses, HSA/401k — all green, no changes.
2. **Don't monetize the personal finance app** (or any side project) while on H-1B. Keep it personal-use. If you ever want to productize it: EAD, concurrent H-1B via a properly structured company, or after moving back to India.
3. **Decline GitHub Sponsors / donations** on open-source work; contribute unpaid if you enjoy it.
4. **No paid tutoring**; if teaching matters to you, look at nonprofit volunteer programs or a concurrent H-1B from a school.
5. **Keep trading casual** — avoid a day-trading pattern, especially during work hours.
6. **Check your employer's outside-activity policy** before *any* side arrangement — financial institutions gate this tightly, independent of immigration law.
7. **Audit your public social media** before any visa appointment — nothing implying side work, freelancing, or "my app's revenue."
8. **If your visa stamp is expired, plan India trips around consular backlogs** (in-person interview mandatory) and re-check fee/proclamation status the week you book.
9. **Watch (as of Sept 2026):** First Circuit merits ruling on the $100K fee; whether the proclamation is extended past Sept 20, 2026; the proposed $103,265 cap-subject fee rule; any DHS follow-on to the weighted lottery.
10. **File clean taxes** categorizing passive income correctly; keep records proving passive activities are passive (property-manager contracts, creation dates of any IP).
