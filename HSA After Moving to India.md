# HSA After Moving to India

> Last updated: 2026-09-18 · Verified against current law (IRS Pub 969/502, OBBBA HSA changes, India Income-tax Act 2025)

**Who this is for:** Indian citizen on H-1B with a US HSA (cash + invested fund units), planning a possible permanent move to India. Covers using the HSA from India, both countries' taxation, custodian logistics, and the keep-vs-drain decision. US-side withdrawal scenarios for all accounts live in **"401K HSA Tax Scenarios"**.

> **What changed recently (2025–2026)**
> - **2026 limits (Rev. Proc. 2025-19):** HSA contribution **$4,400 self / $8,750 family** (+$1,000 catch-up at 55). HDHP = deductible ≥ **$1,700/$3,400**, out-of-pocket max ≤ **$8,500/$17,000**.
> - **OBBBA (July 2025), effective 2026** — all of it is *contribution eligibility* (matters only while you're still in the US): ACA **bronze & catastrophic plans are now HSA-qualified** (IRS Notice 2026-5); **direct primary care** memberships up to $150/mo ($300 family) no longer break eligibility and the fees are qualified expenses; pre-deductible **telehealth** safe harbor is permanent. If you ever have a gap between jobs before leaving, a bronze exchange plan now keeps your HSA contribution window open.
> - **India:** the Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect **April 1, 2026** (sections renumbered; substance unchanged). India **still gives the HSA no recognition whatsoever** — see the India section.
> - CA and NJ remain the only two states not conforming to federal HSA treatment (checked 2026).

---

## 1. The one-paragraph answer

Keep the HSA open when you move. You can spend it **tax-free and penalty-free, at any age, from anywhere in the world**, on IRS-qualified medical expenses — Indian hospital bills, prescriptions, dental, vision all count. You just can't contribute anymore (no US HDHP). The two real threats are: (a) a **non-qualified withdrawal before 65** costs ordinary US tax **plus a 20% penalty**, and (b) once you're **ROR in India**, India taxes the HSA's growth annually like a plain foreign brokerage account and gives no credit for the US penalty. So: spend it on medical care (or banked receipts), front-load withdrawals into the **RNOR window**, and if a balance remains long-term, invest it to minimize annual Indian tax drag.

## 2. Keeping the account: what changes on the move

| | Before move (US resident) | After move (NRA in India) |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Yours | Still yours — no forced closure in tax law |
| Contributions | Up to $4,400/$8,750 (2026) with HDHP | **None** (no HDHP; not an eligible individual). Pro-rate the final year's limit by months of HDHP coverage — overcontributions get a 6% excise until removed |
| Growth | Tax-free (federal) | Still tax-free **to the IRS**; taxable to **India** once ROR |
| Qualified withdrawals | Tax-free | Tax-free — residency-blind |
| Non-qualified withdrawals (<65) | Income + 20% penalty | Same, and it's **US-source income** — files on 1040-NR |
| Age 65+ non-medical | Income, no penalty | Same (works like a Traditional IRA) |

## 3. Qualified expenses incurred in India

The IRS test is *what* the expense is, not *where*. Foreign care is eligible under the same rules as US care (Pub 502 / HSA Store guidance):

| Expense in India | HSA-qualified? |
|---|---|
| Doctor/specialist visits, hospitalization, surgery, lab tests, physiotherapy | ✓ |
| Dental treatment, vision exams, glasses/contacts | ✓ |
| Prescription medicines bought **and consumed** in India, legal in **both** India and the US | ✓ — the "legally procured" rule |
| Medicines shipped/carried from India into the US | ✗ — imported drugs generally don't qualify |
| Ayurvedic/alternative treatments, supplements, general wellness | ✗ unless prescribed treatment for a diagnosed condition (gray area — keep a doctor's note) |
| **Indian health insurance premiums** | ✗ — premiums are never qualified (narrow exceptions: COBRA, premiums while on US unemployment, LTC insurance, Medicare at 65) |
| Cosmetic procedures | ✗ |
| Expenses for spouse/dependents (even non-US persons) | ✓ if the expense itself qualifies |

Qualified expenses for the 20%-penalty-free treatment must be incurred **after the HSA was established** — your establishment date is fixed and travels with you (preserve it: never let the balance hit zero if your state's trust rules matter, and do transfers, not close-and-reopen).

## 4. Paying and reimbursing from India

**Debit card:** HSA debit cards are Visa/Mastercard and generally work at Indian hospitals and pharmacies that take cards. Expect a **1–3% foreign-transaction fee** and INR→USD conversion. Some custodians block foreign point-of-sale by default — test it, or ask. Card-at-POS is the low-friction path for real-time expenses.

**Reimbursement (better for most cases):** pay out of pocket (Indian card/UPI), then transfer from the HSA to your **US bank account** as a "normal distribution." No claim adjudication happens — HSAs are honor-system; *you* prove qualification only if audited. Record for each expense: date, provider, diagnosis/service, amount in INR, USD value at that day's exchange rate, and an English translation if the bill isn't already in English.

**The shoebox strategy (unlimited deferral):** there is **no deadline** to reimburse yourself (IRS Notice 2004-50 Q&A-39). Pay medical costs out of pocket for years, keep the receipts, let the HSA compound, and withdraw the accumulated total whenever you want cash — tax-free. Banked receipts are effectively a **tax-free ATM balance**. Two cross-border wrinkles:
- Receipts must post-date the HSA's establishment.
- Reimburse **before you become ROR** if possible — once ROR, India may treat even a US-tax-free reimbursement as taxable income (next section). Receipts banked during RNOR are best cashed during RNOR.

## 5. US taxation as a nonresident alien

- **Qualified distributions: $0 US tax, $0 penalty — residency doesn't matter.**
- **Non-qualified before 65:** the distribution is **US-source income** (pre-tax US wages funded it), taxed at graduated NRA rates on Form 1040-NR — remember NRAs get **no standard deduction** — plus the **20% additional tax**. No treaty relief: an HSA is not a "pension" under DTAA Article 20; it falls under Other Income, taxable by both countries. Example: cash out $30,000 non-qualified at 35 → ~$3.4k US income tax + $6.0k penalty ≈ **31% gone**, before India.
- **Withholding:** HSA custodians typically do **not** withhold on distributions (you'll get a 1099-SA; some may issue 1042-S for foreign addresses). No withholding ≠ no tax — you self-assess on 1040-NR.
- **Filing rule:** you **must file Form 1040-NR + Form 8889 for any year you take any HSA distribution**, even a fully qualified one (the 1040-NR instructions require it). No distributions in a year → no US filing for the HSA. Your SSN remains your filing ID.
- **At 65:** the 20% penalty disappears; non-medical withdrawals become ordinary income only (IRA-like). Medical stays tax-free for life.

## 6. India's taxation — India does not recognize the HSA

Once you are **ROR** (typically 2–3 fiscal years after return — see the RNOR table in the 401K doc):

- India treats the HSA as an ordinary **foreign investment account**. Interest and dividends are taxable **annually as they accrue**; capital gains on the invested fund units are taxable when realized (including internal rebalancing/sales) — all at slab/capital-gains rates, regardless of the US shelter.
- **Withdrawals:** the conservative practitioner position is that distributions are taxable in India except to the extent they represent already-taxed amounts; India gives **no deduction for personal medical expenses**, so "it was for a hospital bill" doesn't help. Positions vary on whether principal is taxed — genuinely unsettled; a cross-border CA should track your India-side basis.
- **Section 89A relief (now §158 under the 2025 Act) very likely does NOT cover HSAs** — it applies to "retirement benefits accounts," and an HSA isn't one. Flagged as an uncertain/aggressive position if anyone suggests otherwise.
- **Disclosure:** the HSA must appear in **Schedule FA** of your Indian ITR every year you're ROR. Non-disclosure risks Black Money Act penalties (₹10 lakh per year per asset). Non-negotiable.
- **No credit for the 20% penalty:** India's FTC covers US income taxes, not penalty/additional taxes. A non-qualified withdrawal while ROR can approach **45–55% total loss**.
- **While NRI/RNOR:** none of the above bites — HSA growth and withdrawals are foreign income, exempt in India. **The RNOR window is when HSA money moves cheapest.**

## 7. State taxes (CA/NJ)

- **While a CA or NJ resident:** contributions are not deductible for state tax and annual HSA earnings (interest/dividends/realized gains) are state-taxable each year — CA via Schedule CA adjustments. This is about your remaining US years, and it's a real (small) annual cost of holding an aggressive HSA portfolio in CA.
- **After moving to India:** you are a nonresident with no CA/NJ-source income; **no state tax on HSA growth or withdrawals**, and no state filing. (4 U.S.C. §114 doesn't technically cover HSAs, but it doesn't need to — there's simply no residency nexus.) Take any large realizations *after* state residency cleanly ends.

## 8. Custodian logistics — where accounts actually get stuck

Policies, not tax law, are the practical risk. As of 2026:

| Custodian | Reported posture for foreign addresses |
|---|---|
| **Fidelity HSA** | Keeps accounts with foreign addresses; blocks **new** US mutual fund purchases and new contributions; existing holdings stay. Generally the most expat-tolerant; note Fidelity's *retail brokerage* stance on India residents is stricter (can go liquidation-only) — HSA experiences vary, confirm in writing |
| **HealthEquity** | Card usable abroad where Visa is accepted (their help center confirms foreign use + FX fees); employer-tied accounts move to individual pricing after you leave the job |
| **Optum Bank / HSA Bank** | Historically require a US address in practice; foreign-address support inconsistent — highest risk of forced closure/checks mailed to stale addresses |
| Lively and other fintech HSAs | Explicitly document keeping HSAs abroad; investment menus may be restricted |

*(These are observed policies, not law — they change without notice. Get your custodian's current answer in writing before you leave.)*

Checklist to make the account survivable from India:
- **Consider a trustee-to-trustee transfer to Fidelity HSA before leaving** (no tax, no limit on transfers; avoid the 60-day rollover method).
- Keep a **US mailing address** (relative) on file, a **US phone number on Google Voice** for 2FA, and a **US bank account** as the distribution landing zone.
- Confirm: online access from foreign IPs, debit card reissue abroad, W-8 handling, e-delivery of 1099-SA/5498-SA.
- If a custodian force-closes anyway, the check is a distribution — you have **60 days** to redeposit into a new HSA to avoid tax + 20%.

## 9. Strategy by scenario

| Situation | Best play |
|---|---|
| Staying in the US | Max contributions ($4,400/$8,750 in 2026), invest, pay medical out of pocket, bank receipts. If between jobs in 2026+, a bronze exchange plan keeps eligibility (OBBBA) |
| Leaving within a year | Pro-rate final-year contribution; transfer to an expat-friendly custodian; batch any pending medical/dental/vision work while still insured; export all receipt history |
| Just returned (NRI/RNOR years) | **Prime window.** Reimburse every banked receipt; run qualified expenses through the HSA freely; if the balance is small (roughly < $10–15k), consider fully draining it via receipts + qualified spending before ROR to escape India's annual tax drag entirely |
| ROR with a large remaining balance | Keep it as the family's dollar medical fund: qualified withdrawals stay US-tax-free forever. Accept/minimize Indian tax on growth (lower-turnover, lower-yield holdings), declare in Schedule FA, track India-side basis |
| Tempted to cash out non-qualified | Don't before 65 — 31%+ to the US alone, ~half if ROR. At 65+ it's income-tax-only (and during a low-income year, cheap) |
| Age 65+ | Penalty gone. Use for anything (taxed) or medical (tax-free). Medicare premiums become qualified if you're ever back in the US system |

## 10. Action items / decision points

**Now (still in the US):**
- [ ] Start/maintain the receipt shoebox: every medical, dental, vision, pharmacy receipt since the HSA's establishment date, stored digitally with dates and USD amounts.
- [ ] Record the HSA **establishment date** somewhere permanent.
- [ ] Max 2026 contributions if HDHP-covered ($4,400 self).

**Before departure:**
- [ ] Written confirmation from custodian on foreign-address policy — or transfer to Fidelity HSA (trustee-to-trustee).
- [ ] US address, Google Voice number, US bank account all in place; test online login + a small distribution end-to-end.
- [ ] Pro-rate the final-year contribution by HDHP months; remove any excess before the filing deadline.
- [ ] Complete elective medical/dental/vision care while still covered; pay via HSA or bank the receipts.
- [ ] Name/refresh the HSA **beneficiary** (a non-spouse beneficiary takes the full balance as taxable income in one year; the balance also counts toward the $60k NRA US estate exposure).

**In India — RNOR years:**
- [ ] Cash out all banked receipts (tax-free US, exempt India). File **1040-NR + 8889** for each distribution year.
- [ ] Decide: drain small balance now vs. keep as long-term dollar medical fund.
- [ ] Use the card/reimbursement flow for real Indian medical costs; keep bilingual documentation with exchange-rate notes.

**In India — once ROR:**
- [ ] Add the HSA to **Schedule FA** every year; report interest/dividends/gains to India annually; engage a cross-border CA on the withdrawal-basis question.
- [ ] Rebalance toward low-distribution holdings to cut Indian tax drag.
- [ ] Never take a non-qualified distribution before 65.

**Watch list:** custodian policy changes for foreign addresses; any India CBDT clarification on HSAs/89A-§158 scope; any move to extend HSA eligibility to non-US insurance (none as of Sep 2026); India budget for inheritance-tax noise.
